# Stratechery

Stratechery is the Substack publication by Ben Thompson covering the intersection of platform strategy, technology business models, and policy. From 2013 onward, Stratechery has been one of the most-read independent publications tracking platform economics; the corpus's coverage of AI policy is enriched by Thompson's regular writing on how frontier AI business models differ from prior platform waves.

In the Polycrisis corpus, Stratechery contributes the **AI as "frontier firm"** frame — the analytic argument that AI is a platform-shifting technology akin to the database or the browser, but with novel characteristics (capabilities scale with inference cost, not with user count; the underlying model is opaque to downstream integrators; distribution advantages tilt the field toward incumbents). Stratechery's analysis is one of the principal references for why a Polycrisis simulation that reduces AI policy to "will this model be banned or permitted" is missing the deeper dynamics — the platform-shift dynamic is what changes the disposition of capital, the nature of procurement, and the political economy of state intervention.

## In the Polycrisis corpus

Metaviews references to Stratechery focus on the 2023–2025 corpus of AI essays, with specific attention to Thompson's analysis of OpenAI's relationship with Microsoft, the emergence of Anthropic as a distinct competitor, the OpenAI-broad structural question, the "AI everything app" framing, and the comparison between traditional saas and AI-native business models. The corpus uses Stratechery as the principal reference for *market structure* analyses — the kind of work that distinguishes between regulation that changes AI and regulation that doesn't.

A note on positionality: Stratechery is a perspective, not an institution. The corpus treats Thompson's analysis as a credible analyst's interpretation rather than as institutional fact. Simulations cite Stratechery when they want to claim "this kind of policy analysis exists and is well-regarded" without committing to specific predictions.
