# EU AI Office

The European AI Office is the European Commission's directorate-general-level body established in 2024 to enforce the EU AI Act, the world's first horizontally-applied regulation of AI systems. Located within the Commission's DG CONNECT framework and drawing staff from member states' competent authorities, the AI Office is responsible for (1) classifying general-purpose AI (GPAI) models under the Act's tiered risk framework, (2) coordinating evaluations of GPAI providers including systemic-risk obligations, (3) supervising the implementation of the Act by national authorities, and (4) representing the EU's regulatory voice in international coordination.

In the Polycrisis corpus, the EU AI Office is the principal real-world reference for "AI governance as a continuous operational practice" rather than "AI governance as legislation." The AI Act's text was finalized in 2024 after multi-year negotiation, but the AI Office's actual work — model evaluations, provider consultations, member-state guidance, and international coordination — is what operationalizes the regime. The corpus treats the AI Office as the case study in how a multi-jurisdictional regulator operates when the regulated entities themselves are global.

## In the Polycrisis corpus

Metaviews coverage of the AI Office focuses on three threads. First, the actual operational questions (which GPAI models trigger systemic-risk designations; what the evaluation methodology looks like; how provider appeals work). Second, the geopolitical dimension (the AI Office's relationship to the UK's AISI; joint US-EU coordination under the AI Act / Executive Order 14110 frameworks; tensions with chinese frontier lab governance). Third, the corporate dimension (which EU providers are currently facing obligations; the practical experience of compliance). The AI Act's transparency obligations and provider accountability provisions are core corpus concepts that the AI Office concretizes.

A corpus-level note: the AI Office is one of the few real-world bodies whose creation is itself documented in real time across the Metaviews archive — its structure, leadership, and first enforcement actions are public record. Simulations in Polycrisis often reference the AI Office as the instance of "regulatory body" in advisor voices and crisis frames.
