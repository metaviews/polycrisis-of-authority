# UK AI Safety Institute

The UK AI Safety Institute (AISI) is the British government's frontier-model-evaluation body established in November 2023 at Bletchley Park. Its mission is to conduct pre-deployment evaluations of frontier AI models on a voluntary basis, working with major model developers including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind. The AISI was the first government body specifically dedicated to frontier-model safety testing at the intersection of capability evaluation and policy translation.

In the Polycrisis corpus, the AISI is significant because it (1) operationalizes the concept of "frontier model evaluation as a public-sector activity" rather than purely a private-sector internal review; (2) coordinates internationally — including with the US AI Safety Institute (a parallel body established in 2024 under NIST, renamed from its earlier staff), the EU AI Office, and the Frontier Model Forum; and (3) has assembled what is publicly the most procedurally advanced evaluation toolkit, including the Inspect framework for model evaluations.

## In the Polycrisis corpus

Metaviews coverage of the AISI frames it as the operational reference for "what good government AI oversight looks like" — independently of whether the UK government's political direction is supportive, ambivalent, or hostile. The AISI's procedural outputs (model evaluation reports, capability testing methodology, the Inspect framework) are durable. The corpus also tracks AISI's coordination role: joint evaluations with US and EU counterparts, the November 2023 Bletchley declaration, the November 2024 Seoul summit follow-up, the February 2025 Paris summit continuation.

A corpus-level note: the AISI was renamed / restructured in 2025 — the UK government changed its direction from "Safety" toward "Security" and the institute's formal name shifted. The corpus documents this as a real-world example of how policy continuity is fragile across government administrations. Simulations that reference the AISI do so under the contemporary name for the time period the simulation represents.
