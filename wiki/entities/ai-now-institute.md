# AI Now Institute

The AI Now Institute is a research body housed at New York University (originally founded in 2017) that produces annual reports and policy analysis on the social, economic, and political consequences of AI systems in deployment. Distinguished from industry-aligned policy shops (Frontier Model Forum, Partnership on AI) by its explicit "watchdog" framing and from think-tank-academic policy work (RAND, Brookings, CSET) by its institutional commitment to civil-society positions, AI Now's corpus contribution is principally the **AI Now Annual Report** (one of the field's longest-running and most cited) and more recently the **AI Accountability Database** (a structured index of policy actions, incidents, and litigation across AI governance).

In the Polycrisis corpus, AI Now is the principal reference for the "accountability" frame — a frame that treats AI systems as consequential, the institutions that deploy them as accountable, and the governance conversation as substantive rather than procedural. The institute's leadership (Meredith Whittaker, previously at Google; co-founder of the AIAW coalition) and institutional positioning matter for what arguments the corpus can credibly cite. AI Now's specific contributions to the corpus include the "foundation model ecosystem accountability" frame (analyzing dependencies of the foundation-model layer on the labor, infrastructure, and policy arrangements below); the antitrust argument (concentration of power at the foundation-model layer); and the procurement-policy argument (how government procurement shapes the market).

## In the Polycrisis corpus

Metaviews coverage of AI Now draws on the institute's annual reports — particularly the 2023 report on foundation-model accountability and the 2024 report "Enforcing Accountability in the Foundation Model Ecosystem." The corpus treats AI Now as the principal US-based civil-society voice for AI governance grounded in empirical impact analysis rather than in principle or speculation. The AI Accountability Database is also cited as a corpus anchor — when the simulation wants to reference "real accountability cases" (incidents, legal actions, regulatory responses), it can do so via specific entries the database maintains.

A note on positionality: AI Now's framing is politically specific — the institute has consistently argued for strong regulatory intervention, antitrust enforcement, and labor protections in AI deployment. The Polycrisis corpus treats this framing as a real-world reference but does not endorse it; the simulation surfaces it through advisor voices and crisis frames without taking the project's position.

## Key posts

The archive's AI Now references cluster around: the institute's annual-report cadence (2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024), Meredith Whittaker's public commentary on industry/government coordination, and specific incidents the institute has called out (Amazon's Ring partnership with police, facial recognition deployment cases, the labor conditions of training-data annotators).
