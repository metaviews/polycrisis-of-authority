# DeepSeek

DeepSeek is the chinese frontier AI lab whose January 2025 release of DeepSeek-V3 — a large mixture-of-experts (MoE) language model trained at a substantially lower cost than US-anchored frontier models of comparable capability — became the canonical "Sputnik moment" framing in mainstream AI policy commentary. Founded in 2023 in Hangzhou, operated under the Chinese hedge fund High-Flyer's broader enterprise, and shipping open-weight and API-served models, DeepSeek re-anchored the conversation around training-cost economics rather than raw parameter count.

In the Polycrisis corpus, DeepSeek is significant because it inverts two narratives that had become load-bearing through 2024. The first is the "compute moat" thesis, which argued that US export controls on advanced GPUs (H100s and successors) would structurally slow Chinese frontier AI development. DeepSeek's published training infrastructure details (training-cost disclosures, MoE routing efficiency, attention optimizations) provided evidence that the compute gap was narrower than the export-control thesis assumed. The second is the "openAI-as-benchmark" thesis, which anchored policy and procurement discussion on a single US vendor. DeepSeek's open-weight release created a second option that policymakers and procurement teams could evaluate on capability rather than on national-jurisdiction priors.

## In the Polycrisis corpus

Metaviews coverage of DeepSeek expands across three vectors: (1) the technical story (architecture choices, training-cost disclosures, capability-vs-cost benchmarking), (2) the geopolitical story (DeepSeek as the Sputnik-moment data point in the "AI Cold War" narrative, US export-control efficacy, the implication for allied-procurement policy), and (3) the regulatory story (chinese state-position on AI governance, the MIIT's stance on model releases, the cross-jurisdictional question of how to evaluate open-weight models from competing jurisdictions).

A note of conceptual context: the corpus distinguishes DeepSeek's *model* dimension (which is genuinely novel and competitive) from the *ecosystem* dimension (which is bounded by chinese state internet policy, US export controls, and the procurement policies of allied governments). A simulation in Polycrisis treats DeepSeek as a high-leverage reality-tilting force — a single corporeal actor with outsized impact on AI policy framing in 2025–2026 — without taking a position on whether DeepSeek's long-term sustainability depends on continued state support or capital-market momentum.
