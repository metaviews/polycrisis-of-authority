# Algorithmic Justice League

The Algorithmic Justice League (AJL), founded by Joy Buolamwini while at the MIT Media Lab and launched publicly in 2016, is a US-based civil-society organization focused on the social implications of AI, particularly the way algorithmic systems encode biases that produce unequal outcomes across demographic groups. The AJL's most-cited work is **Gender Shades** (Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018), a peer-reviewed study that documented substantially higher error rates in commercial facial-recognition systems for darker-skinned women compared with lighter-skinned men. The Gender Shades study and the AJL's broader advocacy contributed materially to the subsequent regulatory work on biometric AI in the EU, the US, and Brazil.

The AJL is one of the principal civil-society voices specifically framing AI policy as a civil-rights question. Its position complements AI Now's accountability-focus and FLI's existential-risk-focus: AJL emphasizes the civil-rights dimension specifically, where AI Now emphasizes accountability generally and FLI emphasizes catastrophic outcomes.
