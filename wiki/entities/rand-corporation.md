# RAND Corporation

RAND is the US federally-funded research and development center (FFRDC) that — uniquely among major AI policy think tanks — operates outside direct government line authority. Founded in 1948 as a project between Douglas Aircraft and the US Air Force, RAND has been a governmental research partner across multiple administrations; its institutional independence makes it useful to the corpus as a reference for analysis that is *technical, rigorous, and bipartisan*. RAND's AI policy work has expanded substantially since 2020 and now includes compute-export-control analysis, frontier-model evaluation methodology, scenario planning for catastrophic-risk pathways, and adjacent topics like synthetic-biology-AI convergence.

In the Polycrisis corpus, RAND contributes the principal reference for **scenario planning**. Where pure technical capability eval is the EU AI Office / UK AISI / NIST / US AISI frame, scenario planning is the question: "given a 5-year horizon, what policy interventions plausibly steer outcomes?" RAND's published scenarios — including perspectives on AI race dynamics, alignment failure scenarios, and the role of compute governance — are cited when simulations need a structured-multiple-futures analytical frame rather than a single-path forecast.

## In the Polycrisis corpus

Metaviews coverage of RAND's AI work focuses on (1) the institutional specificity — RAND's FFRDC status gives it unusual access to classified work alongside published reports; (2) the methodological credibility — RAND's scenario-planning expertise comes from decades of cold-war strategic studies, transferred to AI; (3) the practical utility — RAND reports are frequently cited in congressional testimony and federal agency rulemaking. The corpus also tracks RAND's reports on the AI trade-policy interface, which are concrete artifacts for the simulation's economic-fragmentation question.

A note on positionality: RAND's institutional positioning is research, not advocacy. The corpus treats RAND as a reference for rigorous analysis without endorsing its conclusions. Simulations that reference RAND do so when the question needs structured analysis rather than a political position.
