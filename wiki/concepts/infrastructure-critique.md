# Infrastructure Critique

Infrastructure critique in the AI corpus refers to AI Now's framing of structural AI critique — the energy, water, labor, and supply-chain externalities of AI deployment that sit beneath the surface of model development. The framing is distinct from capability-focused critique in that it focuses on inputs and externalities rather than outputs.

The substantive content includes: (1) **Energy externalities** — frontier-model training and inference has substantial energy consumption; the IEA Electricity 2024 report estimates AI's global electricity demand as a fraction of total data-center demand, with substantial regional concentration. (2) **Water externalities** — data-center cooling has substantial water consumption, particularly in arid regions. (3) **Labor externalities** — training-data annotation and content moderation has substantial low-wage labor implications, with documented poor working conditions in some segments. (4) **Supply-chain externalities** — the underlying TSMC fabrication dependency has geopolitical consequences that affect both AI policy and Taiwan security policy.

The AI Now framing has been influential because it shifts the discourse's focus from model capability to model deployment's broader effects. Where capability-focused critique asks "what can this model do?" and "what should we restrict?", infrastructure critique asks "what does building and deploying this model cost in human and environmental terms?"

In the Polycrisis corpus, this concept is the principal reference for the substantive accountability discourse on AI's broader externalities. Crisis frames involving data-center siting decisions, energy policy implications of AI deployment, or labor conditions in the AI industry can be grounded against this concept. Advisor voices framed as civil-society researchers, environmental-policy specialists, and labor researchers can be grounded here.
