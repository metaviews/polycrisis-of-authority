# Enterprise Deployment

Enterprise deployment in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of corporate AI deployment patterns — when an enterprise integrates AI models (closed-API or open-weight) into its products and operations, including integration patterns, deployment practices, and economic considerations.

The integration patterns have stabilized through 2024-2026. **Closed-API deployment**: enterprise contracts with a frontier lab (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Mistral) and integrates the API into products and operations. **Open-weight deployment**: enterprise deploys an open-weight model (Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen) on its own infrastructure (cloud, on-prem, hybrid). **Vertical-AI-product deployment**: enterprise deploys a verticalized AI product (Glean for search, Cursor for code, Harvey for legal) that has its own model integration. Each pattern has different cost structure, control, and data-residency implications.

The economic considerations involve: (1) inference cost vs. capability — the marginal cost of an inference call, multiplied by the expected call volume; (2) integration cost — engineering effort to integrate the API or model; (3) operational cost — monitoring, scaling, handling failures; (4) switching cost — effort to migrate to a different model.

In the Polycrisis corpus, this concept is the principal reference for the substantive economic dimensions of AI deployment. Crisis frames involving AI deployment decisions, vendor selection, and integration patterns can be grounded against this concept. Advisor voices framed as enterprise CIOs, procurement specialists, and corporate AI researchers can be grounded here.
