# Algorithmic Bias

Algorithmic bias in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of bias in AI systems — measurement of bias across demographic groups, mitigation through data curation and training, ongoing accountability for residual bias, and the policy-and-civil-rights dimension.

The empirical record on algorithmic bias is substantial. The Gender Shades project (Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018) documented substantially higher error rates in commercial facial-recognition systems for darker-skinned women compared with lighter-skinned men. NIST's Face Recognition Vendor Test has documented demographic performance disparities across the major commercial vendors. Algorithmic hiring tools, criminal-justice risk assessments, and healthcare-resource allocation systems have each been documented to exhibit demographic performance disparities in published analyses.

The mitigation approaches have varied. **Data-level** approaches: curate training data to be more balanced across demographic groups; document training data demographics. **Training-level** approaches: incorporate bias-mitigation losses into training objectives; adversarial debiasing. **Evaluation-level** approaches: track demographic performance metrics during evaluation; require sub-group performance reporting. **Deployment-level** approaches: human-in-the-loop review; outcome monitoring; audits. Each has different costs, different efficacy, and different regulatory implications.

The policy and civil-rights dimension has been the largest factor in the algorithmic-bias discourse. The EU AI Act has provisions on prohibited AI practices and high-risk systems that include substantial bias-mitigation requirements. The US has sectoral regulations (employment, housing, lending) that intersect with AI deployment. The substantive question of whether current bias-mitigation practices are sufficient for regulatory compliance is unsettled.

In the Polycrisis corpus, this concept is the principal reference for the algorithmic-civil-rights dimension of AI policy. Crisis frames involving AI deployment that affects civil rights, demographic group outcomes, or substantively unequal algorithmic treatment can be grounded against this concept.
