# Open Weights

"Open weights" in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of model release where a frontier lab publishes the trained model weights under a license that allows downstream use, modifying the dynamics of distribution, accountability, and state-strategic positioning. The concept is distinct from "open source" (which includes training data, training code, and the full research artifact), and from "API access" (which keeps weights proprietary while allowing inference access).

The current open-weight frontier includes Meta's Llama family (Llama 2 in 2023, Llama 3 in 2024), Mistral's model families (Mistral 7B, Mixtral 8x7B / 8x22B), DeepSeek's model releases, Alibaba's Qwen family, and Zhipu's GLM line. Each release is under a different license (Llama's community-license, Mistral's Apache 2.0, DeepSeek's MIT-style permissive license) with different constraints on commercial use, redistribution, and downstream modifications.

The substantive policy implications include: (1) the distribution dimension — open-weight releases change who can use frontier capability, including adversarial actors; (2) the accountability dimension — open-weight releases change who is accountable for downstream use; (3) the state-strategic dimension — open-weight releases affect which jurisdictions can build sovereign-AI capacity; (4) the safety dimension — open-weight releases affect what safety commitments can be enforced.

The 2024-2026 debate over open-weight releases has been consequential. Closed-frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) have argued that open-weight release is incompatible with frontier safety commitments. Open-weight advocates (Meta, Mistral, DeepSeek) have argued that broad access is structurally good for distribution and accountability. The DeepSeek-V3 January 2025 release was a turning point: an open-weight model produced at competitive capability and substantially lower cost than US-anchored baselines.
