# AI Act Enforcement

AI Act Enforcement in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of enforcement for the EU AI Act — the operational mechanism by which the Act's binding obligations are operationalized, audited, and enforced. The concept is distinct from the Act's text (which sets obligations) and from the EU AI Office's institutional structure (which coordinates enforcement).

The substantive content includes: (1) **Notification and registration** — providers of high-risk AI systems must register their systems; providers of GPAI models with systemic-risk obligations must notify the AI Office. (2) **Conformity assessment** — providers must complete conformity assessments before deploying high-risk systems; the assessments can be self-assessment or third-party, depending on the system category. (3) **Post-market monitoring** — providers are subject to ongoing post-market monitoring of their high-risk deployments. (4) **Audit and inspection** — competent authorities can audit provider practices; audits include documentation review, technical assessment, and operational review.

The institutional structure: the EU AI Office coordinates enforcement; national competent authorities in each member state handle operational enforcement; the AI Board (composed of member-state representatives) coordinates cross-border cases. Penalties include fines (up to 7% of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited systems), market withdrawal, and injunctive relief.

The empirical record on AI Act enforcement is partial — the Act's enforcement provisions are defined in the text but the operational practice is still emerging (first enforcement actions are expected in 2025-2026). The substantive question of how rigorous the enforcement will be in practice is open.
