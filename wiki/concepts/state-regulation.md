# State Regulation

State regulation in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of state-level AI regulation — binding laws, regulatory agencies, enforcement infrastructure, and the comparative dynamics across jurisdictions. The concept is broader than the EU AI Act or NIST AI RFM specifically; it includes all forms of state-level regulatory intervention.

The substantive content includes: (1) **Binding laws** — sectoral or horizontal legislation that creates specific obligations. The EU AI Act is the canonical example. (2) **Regulatory agencies** — bodies that develop and apply the regulatory regime. The EU AI Office, the UK's AISI successor bodies, China's CAC/MIIT, the US's executive-branch AI coordination office. (3) **Enforcement infrastructure** — the mechanisms by which obligations are operationalized (audits, fines, court actions, certification regimes). (4) **Comparative dynamics** — the way different jurisdictions' regulatory interventions interact with each other (Brussels effect, foreign-jurisdiction effects, harmonization efforts).

The 2024-2026 state-regulation landscape is heterogeneous. The EU has the most substantive binding AI law. The US has substantial sectoral regulation (employment, healthcare, education) but no comprehensive AI law. The UK has operational practice (AISI) but limited binding regulation. China has services-registration for generative AI. The jurisdictional variation produces structural complexity for any AI deployment that crosses borders.
