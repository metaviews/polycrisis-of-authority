# Labor Displacement

Labor displacement in the AI corpus refers to the structural substitution of human labor by AI systems, the augmentation of human labor by AI systems, and the political-economy consequences that follow. The concept is empirical — drawn from observed and estimated effects — but has substantial normative content because the question of which displacement is good or bad depends on the distributional effects.

The empirical state of labor displacement through 2025-2026 is partial and uneven. Direct substitution has been observed in customer-service (Klarna's announced AI customer-service, with subsequent rollback for quality reasons), some clerical and entry-level legal work, some medical imaging triage. Augmentation is more common — most knowledge workers use AI tools that produce productivity effects, but the displacement is smaller. The macroeconomic productivity effect has been modest — NBER w31161 (Acemoglu and Restrepo) provides the substantive estimate; the productivity-paradox remains real.

The structural question is not just whether displacement happens but how. Substitution at the lowest tier of professions produces a "barbell" labor market (high-skill work augmented, low-skill work augmented, mid-skill work substituted). Augmentation that increases per-worker productivity produces wage effects that vary substantially depending on the institutional setting — wage increases in tight markets, wage stagnation or decreases in loose markets. Direct substitution in specific applications (Klarna's call-center AI) produces specific quality issues that are not captured in productivity statistics.

In the Polycrisis corpus, this concept is the structural reference for simulation frames involving labor displacement. Crisis frames that invoke substitution (a deployment of AI replacing workers) or augmentation (AI embedded in workers' tools) can be grounded against this concept. Advisor voices framed as labor economists, AI-workforce researchers, and sociologists on AI's effects on work can be grounded here.

The simulation can be used to explore which substitution scenarios produce which political coalitions, which augmentation scenarios produce durable wage gains, and which direct-substitution scenarios produce quality issues that reverse the deployment.
