# Chinese Policy

Chinese policy in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of China's overall AI policy stance — the regulatory regime, industrial-strategy direction, geopolitical positioning, and the institutional structure through which Chinese AI policy is developed and implemented.

The substantive content of Chinese AI policy has four principal dimensions. First, **regulatory content moderation**: services-registration, content-moderation practices, alignment with "core socialist values." Second, **industrial strategy**: substantial state investment in frontier AI capability, sovereign-AI positioning, infrastructure support for chinese-frontier vendors, talent policy. Third, **geopolitical positioning**: state-AI strategy as a component of broader US-China competition, with implications for export controls, foreign-jurisdiction procurement, and the strategic-deconfliction question. Fourth, **institutional structure**: the CAC + MIIT joint administration of services-registration; the broader State Council AI policy coordination.

The empirical record through 2024-2026 shows substantive Chinese policy activity across all four dimensions. The substantive question of how Chinese AI policy interacts with US export controls and allied-state AI procurement is a principal focus of the corpus.

In the Polycrisis corpus, this concept is the principal reference for the Chinese dimension of AI policy.
