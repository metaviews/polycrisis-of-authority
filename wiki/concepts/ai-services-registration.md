# AI Services Registration

## What the registration process actually requires

The operational mechanics: providers file an application with the CAC, including the model's technical specifications, intended use cases, content-moderation practices, and core-socialist-values compliance. CAC reviews the application; approved providers are added to a publicly-available list. The substantive content of these reviews is largely not public.

The substantive regulatory regime combines: (1) content moderation requirements; (2) alignment with "core socialist values"; (3) handling of user disputes; (4) data-provenance documentation; (5) ongoing compliance audits. The substantive content of each is set by the CAC/MIIT jointly.

The empirical record of services-registration through 2024-2026 shows substantial compliance infrastructure. Major Chinese providers — Baidu's ERNIE, Alibaba's Qwen-chat, Zhipu's GLM, and others — have obtained services-registration, with the substantive content of their compliance regimes a mixture of automated content moderation, human content moderation, and intervention pipelines. The compliance infrastructure itself has become a substantive industry capability.

The services-registration regime interacts with the broader Chinese AI industrial policy. Providers that successfully complete registration are eligible for state procurement; providers that fail or decline to seek registration cannot offer services to public-sector users. The procurement-power effect of services-registration is substantial and contributes to the sovereign-AI positioning of the Chinese frontier.

In the Polycrisis corpus, this concept provides the Chinese-jurisdiction counterpart to the EU AI Act (binding), NIST AI RMF (voluntary US), and UK AISI (operational). The substantive framework of services-registration is operational rather than binding in the EU sense (the regulator reviews and approves), but produces durable compliance infrastructure in the Chinese context. Crisis frames involving Chinese AI deployment, services-registration outcomes, or cross-jurisdiction regulatory comparison can be grounded against this concept.
