# Data Provenance

Data provenance refers to the structural question of where AI training data comes from, who owns it, whether its use is consented to, and how its lineage can be traced. The concept applies to all data-driven AI; the 2024-2026 era has put substantial pressure on data-provenance practices as copyright litigation, regulatory disclosure requirements, and consent-organization work have all advanced.

The substantive content falls into three categories. **Origin**: where did the training data come from? Public web crawls, licensed content purchases, user-interaction data, synthetic data, and other sources each produce different provenance profiles. **Ownership**: who has rights over the training data? The copyright holders, the data brokers, the licensing intermediaries, the model developer, the user community, and other stakeholders each have different rights claims. **Use rights**: for what purposes can the data be used? Training is a specific use; other uses (generating similar data, deletion on request, specific-purpose limitations) have specific contractual and regulatory structures.

The policy discourse has three major dimensions. First, **copyright disputes**: NYT v. OpenAI, the Authors Guild v. OpenAI litigation, the substantial class-action work. Second, **regulatory disclosure requirements**: the EU AI Act's data-summary requirements; sectoral privacy laws (GDPR, CPRA) extended to AI; data-broker transparency rules. Third, **consent organization**: the structured efforts to give individuals and rights-holders substantive control over their data's use in AI training.
