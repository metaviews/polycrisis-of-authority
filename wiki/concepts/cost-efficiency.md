# Cost Efficiency

Cost efficiency in the AI corpus refers to the structural concept of training-cost economics in frontier AI development — the relationship between compute, data, and capability, and how that relationship has shifted in 2024-2026.

The pre-2025 baseline: frontier-model training costs were substantially escalating. Each major capability advance required a meaningful increment over the prior cohort. The export-control thesis (US restrictions on advanced compute to China) was grounded in part on the assumption that the cost escalation would compound — restricting access to advanced compute would structurally slow Chinese frontier development.

The 2024-2026 cost-efficiency shift: substantial gains in training efficiency have been documented, with frontier-capability models achievable at meaningfully lower training cost than the prior baseline. DeepSeek-V3's published training cost (~5-6 million dollars in compute, substantially below the prior frontier baseline) was the canonical evidence. The MoE-architecture optimizations, attention-mechanism improvements, and post-training efficiency work each contributed. The substantive implication is that the compute-cost escalation has slowed substantially.

The cost-efficiency shift interacts with the **export-control thesis**. The original thesis posited that export controls on advanced compute would delay Chinese frontier development by years. The empirical evidence through 2025-2026 is that the delay is smaller than expected, with DeepSeek-V3 and subsequent Chinese-frontier outputs advancing despite the controls. The cost-efficiency shift is part of the reason.
