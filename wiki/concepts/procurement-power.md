# Procurement Power

Procurement power is government's structural leverage through what it buys. The concept applies to any state or multilateral institution that has substantial purchasing requirements; the lever is the substantive content of purchase requirements (specific evaluation standards, specific audit practices, specific transparency requirements) and the volume of purchases (large enough to influence vendor practice).

The AI application is structural. Federal AI procurement amounts to substantial sums (the US federal government's discretionary AI spend is several billion dollars annually; allied-state procurement is comparable in scale). The procurement rules can specify model evaluation requirements, transparency practices, supply-chain provenance requirements, safety commitments, and other substantive obligations. A coherent federal AI procurement system can shape the entire frontier-lab business model — most frontier labs have substantial public-sector revenue, and procurement requirements that bind federal use can become de facto industry standards.

The empirical evidence through 2025-2026 is partial. The US OMB M-24-10 memorandum (March 2024) and the AI Playbook for the UK Government (2024) are the principal procurement-rule instruments. The EU is moving toward coordinated AI procurement among member states. The OECD AI Policy Observatory has been tracking multi-jurisdiction procurement.
