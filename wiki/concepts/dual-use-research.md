# Dual-Use Research

Dual-use research concerns, in the AI policy corpus, build on the prior tradition from biology, chemistry, and nuclear-physics research: how to support science that has both beneficial and harmful uses, without constraining the science so much that the beneficial uses are foreclosed. The prior tradition's institutional mechanisms — Institutional Biosafety Committees, Dual Use Research of Concern committees, NSABB review — provide a structural reference even when applied to AI.

The AI-specific dual-use dimensions include: (1) biology and chemistry capabilities (large language models with chemistry expertise can guide synthesis pathways); (2) cyber-offensive capability (LLMs with code-execution can substantially improve the productivity of offensive cyber operations); (3) persuasion and influence (synthetic media produces capabilities for political manipulation at scale); (4) CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) research guidance, where the public evidence is more limited but the concern is real. Each of these dual-use dimensions has different policy responses and different institutional mechanisms.

The structural question of access is the principal policy lever. The biology tradition has substantial review mechanisms and can constrain publication of certain results (the H5N1 ferret-transmissibility case being the most-cited recent example). The AI tradition has had voluntary commitments rather than formal review, with most frontier labs having adopted "responsible scaling" policies that include some dual-use considerations, but the formal review apparatus is much less developed.
