# Talent Concentration and Labor Conditions

The AI talent pool is concentrated in a small number of frontier labs and research institutions. The 2024-2026 distribution of research-active AI scientists is heavily concentrated in (in order of size) US frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta FAIR, Microsoft Research), the Chinese frontier (DeepSeek, Alibaba, Zhipu, Moonshot), and a smaller set of academic and policy-research institutions (Stanford, MIT, CMU, Berkeley, ETH Zurich, MILA, Oxford, the UK AISI, the EU AI Office).

The 2024 wave of senior talent movements (e.g., Inflection's team to Microsoft, the OpenAI departures of 2023-2024, the DeepMind-to-Anthropic movements, the xAI-OpenAI talent exchanges) showed that talent concentration is structural rather than incidental. The 2025-2026 talent landscape is increasingly framed as a state-strategic variable: the US administration's export-control and visa policies, the UK's AI talent visa, the Chinese state's talent-recruitment programs, and the European AI Office's stated goal of "European AI talent" all interact with the talent distribution.

The labor conditions of AI workers are also a substantive concern. The visible tier of senior researchers and engineers has substantial compensation packages; the broader tier of data annotators, RLHF contractors, and content-moderation contractors has been documented as having poor working conditions and low pay. The contrast between these two tiers — within the same industry, working on the same products — is one of the substantive AI-accountability concerns.
