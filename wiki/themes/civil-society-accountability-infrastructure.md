# Civil Society Accountability Infrastructure

The accountability question for AI systems — who is answerable when an AI deployment causes harm — has structural answers in civil-society institutions. AI Now Institute (US, founded 2017) produces the annual reports that have been the principal US-based reference for AI accountability analysis. Future of Life Institute (US, with Brussels office) is the policy-research side of the AI safety ecosystem. Partnership on AI (multi-stakeholder nonprofit) is the broadest accountability work, with industry, civil society, and academic participants. Algorithmic Justice League (US, founded 2016 by Joy Buolamwini) is the principal civil-society voice on algorithmic bias specifically.

The institutional effectiveness of these actors is structural, not optional. The civil-society accountability work that preceded AI — environmental impact research, consumer product safety, civil rights enforcement — produced durable policy effects. The AI accountability work has produced some (the AI Now reports have been cited in Congressional testimony and the EU AI Act negotiations) but is at a much earlier stage.

The interaction between civil-society and state regulation is non-trivial. Civil-society actors can be (a) substitute for state regulation (when state action is absent), (b) complement to state regulation (when civil-society work sharpens what state regulation asks for), (c) critique of state regulation (when state action is captured by industry), or (d) target of state action (when the civil-society position is itself politically contentious). The AI accountability ecosystem is currently in a mix of (a), (b), and (c) — civil-society actors fill gaps the state has not addressed, sharpen state regulation where it exists, and critique state action where it is captured.
