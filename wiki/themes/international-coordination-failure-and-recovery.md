# International Coordination Failure and Recovery

The multilateral AI governance conversation has produced a series of summit declarations, voluntary principles, and coordination frameworks whose actual effect on private-sector behavior has been modest. The Bletchley Park Declaration (November 2023) on frontier AI safety; the Seoul Declaration (May 2024); the Paris AI Action Summit (February 2025); the OECD AI Principles (first adopted 2019, updated 2024); the UN AI Advisory Body's "Governing AI for Humanity" report (September 2024). Each produced a document; the documents share substantial content. Their effect on actual AI development, deployment, or procurement practice has been more limited.

This is not a failure specific to AI; it is the standard failure mode of multilateral voluntary coordination. Climate summits produce similar documents; arms-control negotiations produce similar frameworks; the question is always whether voluntary commitments translate into specific measurable behavior, or whether they remain at the level of shared rhetoric. AI is following a familiar pattern.

The recovery conditions are also familiar. Multilateral coordination tends to recover when (a) a concrete failure (a major AI accident, a public incident) creates political will for binding instruments; (b) a state-jurisdiction establishes binding instruments unilaterally, creating pressure for allied-jurisdiction adoption (the EU AI Act's effect on UK and US conversations is the current example); (c) procurement coordination reaches critical mass among allied states, creating commercial incentives for vendors to comply with a shared framework.
